## Contract exercised

The spike compares fixed cadence (A), Scene one-shot permits (B), and competing
Scene+Depth permits (C), on the host and under `taskset -c 0-3` and
`taskset -c 0-1`. The scheduler mode is runtime-only diagnostics; it does not
mutate `FusedSceneFrame`, `Config`, Fusion state, or durable semantic history.

The binary now reports direct extra-run counts, request outcomes, frame-based
dispatch/resolution waits, and bounded Human/Fusion p50/p95 measurements.

## Gate interpretation

- The same-hardware ≤5% wall-time/capture-throughput gate passes for B and C
  against A at all three CPU budgets (largest observed wall delta: +0.04 s,
  +0.31%).
- B and C produce direct extra Scene executions; C also produces one extra Depth
  execution in every CPU budget. Requests have explicit dispatch, resolution,
  and expiry outcomes; no accepted request is silently treated as resolved.
- Human/Fusion latency is measured directly. Under 2 cores, adaptive C has a
  Human p95 of 121.473 ms versus fixed A at 112.532 ms; this is a latency cost
  of granting heavy work, although capture throughput remains within the gate.
- The bounded 300-frame window truncates some accepted requests before their
  source result arrives. Resolution percentages must not be read as steady-state
  recall; longer runs are required for a stable resolution-rate estimate.

## Decision

Keep the existing `std::thread` AgentMesh and temporary one-shot permits. Do not
add Tokio or a general pool in this spike. The evidence supports a follow-up
bounded heavy-job execution-pool evaluation because C adds Scene+Depth evidence
without a throughput regression, but that pool is not implemented or implied by
this checkpoint. Human/body-primary remains protected and canonical facts remain
immutable.
